# Phase-I method snapshot

AlloEvolve treats the quantum algorithm itself as the object of evolutionary search.

## Candidate genome

A candidate is assembled from typed components:

- residue representation
- interaction/contact weighting
- Hamiltonian or generator
- perturbation
- source state
- evolution/regime parameters
- observable
- residue decoder
- numerical parameters

Ordinary mutation and crossover are combined with LLM-guided semantic proposals.

## Scientific promotion gates

Candidate generation is intentionally more permissive than biological evaluation.

1. **Compatibility** — structurally invalid component combinations are rejected.
2. **Mathematical/regime certification** — architecture-changing mutations invalidate inherited certificates. A promoted candidate must identify the claimed quantum-specific term, a matched resource-off/dephased or commuting limit, and an operationally active parameter regime.
3. **Resource feasibility** — a candidate must retain a credible simulation and hardware realization path.
4. **Challenge readiness** — a promoted candidate must preserve a residue-level decoder and a route to the required `N x N` connectivity matrix and exact ranked top-5 residue prediction.

Unsupported but scientifically interesting ideas remain in a research nursery until recertified.

## Evaluation contract

Three verdicts are kept separate:

- **Challenge viability**
- **Predictive utility**
- **Quantum attribution**

Predictions are frozen before biological labels are joined. Matched controls use the same representation and decoder wherever possible. Positive matched-control residuals are evidence, not proof, of quantum attribution.

## Portfolio search

AlloEvolve preserves:

- broad generalists
- unique/marginal-rescue specialists
- control-adjusted gains
- robust candidates
- behaviorally distinct mechanisms

The objective is a complementary expert portfolio rather than one global winner.

## Applicability research

In parallel, label-blind apo-derived protein/source traits are used to test whether specialist benefit, generalist sufficiency, or persistent failure occupies reproducible regions of protein space.

The intended deployment policy is conservative:

- use a specialist only when prospective evidence supports it;
- use combinations only when validated;
- otherwise fall back to the strongest evolved generalist.
